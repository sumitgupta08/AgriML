[WHEAT IRRIGATION & WATER MANAGEMENT]
Optimal soil moisture for wheat ranges between 45% and 65%. 
If soil moisture drops below 30% during the crown root initiation (CRI) or flowering stage, schedule immediate irrigation within 24 hours.
Avoid flood irrigation if rainfall forecast exceeds 25mm in the next 48 hours to prevent waterlogging and root rot.

[RICE WATER & NUTRIENT MANAGEMENT]
Rice is semi-aquatic and requires high soil moisture, ideally maintained between 70% and 85%.
If soil moisture falls below 50%, water stress occurs, leading to reduced tillering.
Apply Nitrogen in split doses: basal, active tillering, and panicle initiation. 
Avoid urea application when rainfall is imminent to minimize fertilizer runoff and leaching.

[SOIL pH & NUTRIENT AVAILABILITY]
Soil pH between 6.0 and 7.5 provides optimal nutrient uptake for most cereal crops.
When pH is below 5.5 (strongly acidic), phosphorus fixation occurs, making P unavailable to plants. Apply agricultural lime (calcium carbonate).
When pH exceeds 8.0 (alkaline), zinc and iron micronutrient deficiencies are common. Apply gypsum or elemental sulfur.

[NPK DEFICIENCY IDENTIFICATION]
Nitrogen (N) deficiency appears as yellowing (chlorosis) of older, lower leaves while younger leaves stay green.
Phosphorus (P) deficiency causes dark green leaves with purplish or reddish discoloration along leaf margins.
Potassium (K) deficiency results in marginal leaf scorch, yellowing of leaf tips, and weak stalk strength.
